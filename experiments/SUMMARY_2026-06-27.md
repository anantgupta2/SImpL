Everything known so far on the **nbmarg** recipe (the current canonical SImpL variant: per-passage,
all-Q **uniform** understanding marginal — no difficulty weighting / baseline pass / rotate), vs
**cot** (plain CoT-only, 8 reasoning samples) and **cot16** (CoT-only with 16 reasoning samples =
the *compute-matched control*, since nbmarg also spends ~16 generations/step).

Metric: `cot_accuracy`, avg@8, deterministic eval. Δ = vs cot at the same fixed step. Steps 184 and
216 are the agreed fixed reporting points (NOT per-method or cot-convergence selection — that view was
rejected as either cherry-picking or cot-biased). Reading at two fixed steps guards against tail noise.

## The story

**4B: the understanding objective genuinely helps.** nbmarg beats cot AND its compute-matched control
cot16 on all three distinct hard datasets (LSAT-AR, RACE-C, QuAIL). cot16 ≈ 0 everywhere, so the gain
is NOT from extra samples — it's the understanding co-training. This is the positive SImpL result.

**8B: the win shifts from understanding to raw compute.** Under the retuned HP (clip1/lr32, which we
introduced because the old clip05 8B looked under-clipped / converged too low), cot16 (+1.97 on LSAT)
*overtakes* nbmarg (+0.57). Since cot16 is nbmarg's compute control, this says at 8B the edge is the
16 generations, not the understanding. The cot16 8B-LSAT win is NOT a single hot seed — 3 of 4 seeds
≥30.8 @216 (33.48/30.82/29.46/31.96). RACE-50 is saturated (~84.7, all methods tied) at both scales.

**Net framing:** scale-dependent, and sharper than "SImpL helps small models." At 4B the understanding
objective beats its compute control; at 8B it does not, and plain extra sampling does better. The
clip1/lr32 retune raised the 8B floor (cot 28.99→29.46) as hypothesized but did not produce a SImpL win.

## Open question / next step

**The 8B HP is not settled.** cot16 outperforming under clip1/lr32 is itself evidence the 8B optimum is
elsewhere; the right next move is a proper 8B HP sweep (clip × lr, possibly β / us), not more seeds at
this single point. Until then the 8B conclusion is "no SImpL win; compute control wins on LSAT."
4B LSAT-25 low-data (does the nbmarg edge widen with less data?) is n=1 and inconclusive — needs 3 seeds.
